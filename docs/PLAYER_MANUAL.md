# Chronolab Puzzlebox — Player's Manual

Welcome to the Chronolab reactor. Something has knocked its systems out of phase, and you
have one job: bring all seven subsystems back online before the clock runs out. Every
subsystem is a different puzzle, they come in a different random order every time you play,
and the faster you clear all seven, the higher your score.

This manual explains how to start a run, the rules that apply throughout, and exactly how to
solve each of the seven puzzles.

---

## 1. Starting a run

When the box is idle, the TM1638 panel (the digit display with 8 buttons) shows **SET** and
the big screen shows the time-limit picker.

1. **Turn rotary encoder 1** to choose your time limit. Turning it right adds time, left
   removes it, in 1-minute steps, from 5:00 up to 30:00. The chosen time is shown live on
   both the big screen and the TM1638 digits.
2. **Press any button on the TM1638 panel** to lock in that time and start the countdown.
3. A 5-second countdown plays (with a beep each second) — get your hands ready.
4. The moment it hits zero, your run begins: the clock starts counting down from your chosen
   limit, and the first puzzle appears.

**Your score is whatever time is left on the clock the instant all seven puzzles are solved.**
Solve faster, score higher. If the clock hits zero before you finish, the run ends there —
whatever you'd already solved doesn't carry over to a future run.

---

## 2. The rules that apply to every puzzle

- **The selection and order are random.** Each run draws six of the seven main puzzles below
  and shuffles them — you might get Tetris first and the memory game last, and one puzzle sits
  this run out. The Finale always comes last, because it depends on something every other
  puzzle gives you (see below).
- **One shared clock.** There's no separate timer per puzzle. Every second ticks off the same
  overall run clock, shown on the TM1638 panel and the big screen.
- **Mistakes cost time, not attempts.** Nothing you do locks you out or ends a puzzle early.
  Getting something wrong simply burns a chunk of your remaining time (each puzzle below says
  how much) and lets you try again immediately.
- **Listen, not just look.** The box uses sound deliberately, not just for beeps. Several
  puzzles tell you "warmer/colder" or "right/wrong" through pitch alone — it's worth paying
  attention to what you hear, especially once your hands and eyes are busy elsewhere.
- **Each puzzle secretly hands you a piece of the ending.** You won't see this happen — just
  know that solving all six main puzzles is what unlocks the Finale.

## 3. The puzzles

### Energiepatroon — Pattern

**What you'll see:** The TM1638 panel lights up a short sequence of its own buttons, one at a
time, each with its own musical note.

**How to play:**
1. Watch (and listen to) the sequence play once.
2. Repeat it back by pressing the same buttons in the same order.
3. Get it right, and it advances. Get it wrong, and the *same* sequence plays again from the
   start — you don't need to relearn it, just watch/listen again.

**Controls:** TM1638 buttons only.

**Tip:** If the lights are hard to see from where you're standing, the sequence is also a
melody — one note per button. You can solve this one by ear alone.

**Mistake cost:** 15 seconds.

---

### Resonantieraster — Resonant Grid

**What you'll see:** A grid of dark tiles on the big touchscreen.

**How to play:**
1. There's a hidden correct order to touch the tiles in — nothing tells you what it is up
   front.
2. Touch any tile. If it's the correct *next* tile, it lights up green and stays locked in.
3. If it's the wrong tile, you'll hear a tone — the pitch tells you how close that tile is to
   the actual next one (higher pitch = closer). Use that to narrow down the right tile.
4. Keep going until every tile is lit.

**Controls:** Touchscreen only.

**Tip:** The first exploratory touch of any tile is free — it's meant to be a measurement, not
a mistake. Only touching the *same wrong tile* again and again starts costing you time.

**Mistake cost:** Small, and only kicks in after a couple of repeat touches on the same wrong
tile (harder difficulty settings punish repeats sooner).

---

### Trillingscijfer — Vibrational Cipher

**What you'll see:** Not much — the panel shows **STBY**, and there's a quiet hum playing.

**How to play:**
1. The hum will suddenly stop. That silence is your cue: a message is about to come through
   the box as a vibration.
2. Put your hand on the box and feel for a series of buzzes. Each digit of the code is sent
   as a group of short pulses — **count the pulses** in each group. One pulse = digit 0, two
   pulses = digit 1, and so on.
3. Once you've felt the whole code, enter it on the TM1638 buttons in order (button 1 = digit
   0, button 2 = digit 1, etc. — same counting logic).
4. Get it wrong, and the box will buzz the same code out again so you can double check it.

**Controls:** Feel the vibration, enter on TM1638 buttons.

**Tip:** This is the one puzzle where sound actively *stops* to tell you something — don't
wait for a beep, the silence itself is the signal.

**Mistake cost:** Retransmission plus a time penalty (5–12 seconds depending on difficulty).

---

### Spectraalresonantie — Spectral Tuner

**What you'll see:** The three rotary encoders' LEDs glowing somewhere between red and green.

**How to play:**
1. Each of the three encoders has its own hidden target position. Turn an encoder and its
   light shifts from red (far off) through amber to green (locked on target) — and you'll
   hear its pitch rise the same way.
2. All three must be green/locked **at the same time**. Here's the catch: if you let one that
   was already locked drift back out, it will nudge the *other two* targets around as
   punishment for celebrating too early.
3. Once all three are simultaneously locked, press **any encoder's button** to confirm.

**Controls:** All 3 rotary encoders (turn to tune, press to confirm).

**Tip:** This is a three-way balancing act, not three separate puzzles — get all three close
before trying to nail any one of them precisely.

---

### Levend Interval — Living Interval

**What you'll see:** The same three encoders as Spectral Tuner, but this time nothing sits
still.

**How to play:**
1. Each encoder's target is quietly drifting back and forth on its own the whole time — you
   won't find a target and leave it; you have to **track it and move with it**.
2. Get an encoder within range of its live target and *hold it there* for a couple of seconds
   (a rising tone confirms you're inside the band the whole time you're holding).
3. Do this for all three encoders, then press any encoder's button to confirm.

**Controls:** All 3 rotary encoders (turn to track, press to confirm).

**Tip:** If you're only reacting to where the target *is*, you're always a step behind — once
you notice which way it's drifting, start moving with it, not just toward it.

---

### Reactoroverbelasting — Tetris

**What you'll see:** A falling-block puzzle on the big screen, controlled from the TM1638
panel.

**How to play:**
1. Blocks fall on their own, one row at a time, on a steady clock. Use the panel to move and
   stack them before they land: **button 1** moves left, **button 2** rotates, **button 3**
   moves right, **button 4** drops the current piece one row early, **button 5** drops it all
   the way down instantly, **button 6** pauses/resumes (also stops the fall clock).
2. Clear full rows to make progress. Clear the target number of rows to solve it.
3. Don't let the stack reach the top. If a new piece has nowhere to spawn, the reactor
   "overloads" — the board clears itself, you get a fresh start, and it costs you time
   straight off the run clock, the same as a mistake in any other puzzle.

**Controls:** TM1638 buttons 1–6. Screen for the board.

**Tip:** Button 4 and button 5 exist for when you already know where a piece is going —
you don't have to wait for the fall clock if you're ready sooner.

---

### Zwaartekrachtlabyrint — Tilt Maze

**What you'll see:** A maze on the big screen with a yellow ball in the top-left corner, a
glowing green core somewhere far away, and a few black holes with red rims.

**How to play:**
1. Tilt the whole box to roll the ball. "Level" is however the box was lying when the
   puzzle started — if that feels off, press any encoder's button to make the current
   position the new level.
2. Roll the ball into the green core to solve it.
3. Avoid the holes. Fall into one and you feel a buzz, lose time, and the ball goes back to
   the start. The holes sit in side passages right off the correct route, so take junctions
   slowly.

**Controls:** Tilt the box. Encoder buttons re-level.

**Tip:** The TM1638 shows **KERN** with how many cells you still are from the core, and its
LEDs fill up as you get closer.

**Mistake cost:** 5–12 seconds per fall, depending on difficulty.

---

### Eindsequentie — Finale

This one only appears after the six drawn puzzles are solved.

**What you'll see:** The TM1638 panel plays back a short sequence — this time made of the
"fragments" the other six puzzles quietly collected along the way.

**How to play:**
1. Watch and listen to the sequence, exactly like Energiepatroon.
2. Repeat it back on the TM1638 buttons.
3. Get it right, and the reactor comes fully back online: a full sound-and-light celebration
   plays, your run is complete, and your final score is whatever time was left on the clock.

**Controls:** TM1638 buttons only.

**Tip:** This is deliberately the easiest, most forgiving puzzle in the run — it's a victory
lap, not a final gate. Take the win.

---

## 4. Scoring and the highscore list

- Your score is the number of **seconds left on the clock** the moment you finish the Finale.
  A faster clear is a higher score — there's no bonus scoring beyond time remaining.
- If your score is good enough for the **top 10**, the box will ask for your initials right
  after you finish.
- **Enter your initials with the three rotary encoders** — encoder 1 sets the first letter,
  encoder 2 the second, encoder 3 the third. Turn each one to cycle A–Z; the letters update
  live on the big screen. Press any TM1638 button to confirm and save your entry.
- High scores are saved permanently on the box and survive a power cycle — the leaderboard
  carries over from one group of players to the next.

## 5. Quick reference card

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| TM1638 buttons 1–7 | Puzzle input (sequence entry, digit entry, Tetris controls) — button 8 is reserved and never used in play |
| TM1638 panel (any button) | Confirm/start at the Setup, Timeout, and Highscore screens |
| Encoder 1 | Time-limit selection (Setup) · tuning dial 1 · initials letter 1 |
| Encoder 2 | Tuning dial 2 · initials letter 2 |
| Encoder 3 | Tuning dial 3 · initials letter 3 |
| Encoder button (any) | Confirm on Spectral Tuner / Living Interval once all three are locked/held · re-level the Tilt Maze |
| Tilting the box | Tilt Maze only |
| Touchscreen | Resonant Grid only |
| Vibration (feel the box) | Vibrational Cipher only |

Good luck stabilizing the reactor.
